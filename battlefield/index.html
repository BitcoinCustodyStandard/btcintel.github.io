      How to read the battlefield

      What this is
      A picture of the Bitcoin order book right now: the buy and sell orders waiting on exchanges, and the trades and forced closures hitting them. Every soldier, wall and explosion stands for real dollars at a real price — press X (or X-ray) to see the numbers. It shows pressure, not the future.
      Soldiers
      Bulls (blue, horned, round shields) stand on resting buy orders below the price; bears (black and ember, spiked, tower shields) on resting sell orders above it. Soldiers per $50 band = 12 × log2(1 + USD ÷ $250k): $250k is 12 soldiers, $1M 28, $4M 49. Orders can be cancelled at any moment, so an army can vanish without a fight.
      Walls: broken or withdrawn
      A band holding at least 4× the median band and $2M becomes a palisade ($10M+: stone). If trades eat it, it is smashed; if it disappears without trades, it sinks into the fog and the feed says “withdrawn” — a wall is only a promise.
      Charges, surges, volleys
      Market orders are grouped per side per second: under $50k an archer volley, $50k to the large-trade setting an infantry surge, above it a cavalry charge (riders = USD ÷ $100k). Charges move the front because they consume the orders in their path.
      Liquidations
      A forced exit of a borrowed bet. Long liquidations are forced selling, so bulls fall; short liquidations are forced buying, so bears fall. $50k+ infantry fall · $150k+ enemy cavalry ride in · $500k+ siege strike · $2M+ trebuchet volley, lightning and a local rout. Exchanges publish only a sample of liquidations, so the true toll is usually higher.
      Routs and ranges
      A side routs when its orders within 1% of price drop 30% or more within a minute, and re-forms when they recover. A side “takes” a $500 marker when price crosses it and stays beyond it for 60 seconds — a recap of ground taken, not a signal.
      Blood on the ground
      Blood (or, on Clean, scorch) soaks the ground at the price where each liquidation hit and dries over about ten minutes, so the terrain doubles as a recent liquidation map. The gore setting changes only the effects, never the data.
      Front line
      A glowing trench at the current BTC price (the median of the venues’ mid-prices). Its near section is now; the far section bends to trace the last three minutes of price, so you can see where the fighting moved. Drag to rotate the map, scroll or pinch to zoom.
      Breakthrough cost
      The price a $25M market sell (left) or buy (right) would reach by consuming the displayed aggregated book. The closer that price, the thinner that side’s defence.
      Armies
      Bulls stand on resting bids, bears on resting asks. Each price band’s formation grows with its displayed order-book liquidity, so a deep book reads as a deep column. Shading on the ground shows the same depth.
      Walls
      Large walls (a $50 price band holding at least 4× the median band and at least $2M) appear as glowing barriers with an oversized champion in front, labelled with size and price.
      Strikes
      Large aggressive trades arc into the front line from the attacking side: green from buyers lifting offers, red from sellers hitting bids.
      Explosions
      Liquidations. A long liquidation bursts inside the bull ranks (forced selling) with a red shockwave; a short liquidation bursts in the bear ranks (forced buying) with a green one. Size scales with the liquidated notional; $1M+ events shake the field, and every liquidation leaves a scorch mark that fades over a minute.
      What it is not
      A prediction. Displayed liquidity can be pulled at any time, liquidation feeds cover OKX, Deribit and Binance (where reachable) — not the whole market, and the liquidation zones are a model estimate.
